# Hero depth effect

## Intro and tuning

The portrait's width-driven sizing stops at a 1440px viewport by default. `Portrait max viewport width (px)` under Portrait & cursor changes this cap and is included as `portrait.maxWidth` in exports. Height-driven cover sizing on tall screens remains intact. The same cap applies throughout the intro scale animation; the WebGL canvas, text and marquee still span the full viewport.

The scene opens through one refracting ripple from a brand-c4 screen. The portrait starts at scale 2.06 and settles to its configured scale by 90% of the ripple progress with cinematic easing. `scene-intro.ts` stores the intro defaults. The settings export includes `portrait`, `liquid`, and `intro`, and Replay rebuilds the entrance from the current controls.

Pointer-driven parallax and liquid strokes wait until the entrance finishes, then start from a fresh cursor sample. Hovering during the intro cannot inject a stale stroke or interrupt the animated layout. The portrait cache remains invalid until both its entrance and ripple finish, including at slow ripple speeds.

Heading and paragraph timing controls expose the heading wipe, star rotation, paragraph start, word duration and stagger. Copyright stays in HTML: its icon fades, lines grow outward, and clipped digits rise from the centre outward (1995 right-to-left, current year left-to-right). Copyright always starts at `headingStart`; its fade duration, line growth duration and digit stagger are also editable and exported. Reduced motion reveals the content immediately.

The WebGL marquee label cycles through Trusted by, Loved by, Designed for, Built for, Created for, Partnered with and Chosen by on each new scroll gesture or direction reversal. The HTML/mobile label remains unchanged. Text and its rule gap continue to use computed HTML styling.

The Hero's Background renders the refined portrait effect directly in WebGL. No iframe, prototype title, status label, or controls appear on the normal page.

Open `/?heroSettings` to load the optional hero controls and reveal the desktop liquid-controls toggle (any value works, e.g. `?heroSettings=1`). The flag dynamically imports the hero settings component and its styles; the liquid panel loads when its toggle is opened. Normal visits show neither control UI and never request either settings panel. The flag is a convenience for tuning, not an authentication mechanism. Changes in the panels are temporary. Update `src/components/Hero/Background/effect-settings.ts` to change the shipped portrait defaults, or `liquid-settings.ts` for the saved liquid defaults.

The saved setup includes blue-only cursor and scroll chromatic effects, fingertip glow, scroll depth zoom, trails, and focus pull. Input in either direction triggers the same depth pulse once per gesture. On reaching its peak (or after 0.55 seconds), the pulse returns to rest even while scroll momentum continues. A 350ms quiet gap re-arms it. Fast gestures in either direction toggle between monochrome and colour once per gesture, with a smooth transition. The generated colour source is served as `portrait-colour.webp` (quality 90, 157KB versus the 1.32MB PNG, same 1508×1043 dimensions). It supplies chroma only: the shader retains original luminance, alpha, silhouette and depth mapping. Colour is an interpretation, not a recovery of the original photograph's colours. Missing colour assets gracefully retain monochrome. Wheel/touch events remain passive and do not block Lenis.

Desktop visitors with a fine pointer and hover support get the Clear water liquid cursor. The existing logo images are rasterised into a strip texture on layout/content changes, then animated behind the transparent depth portrait in the same WebGL context. By default, the fluid pass refracts the portrait and background together. In behind mode, it refracts only the background and logos, then composites the portrait on top using its original alpha. Both modes retain the existing pointer effects, scroll trails and soft edges. Logo content still comes from the original marquee component. Mobile and coarse-pointer visitors keep that component's HTML/CSS animation. The HTML marquee stays visible until the GPU version is ready, and remains the fallback when float textures or their framebuffers are unsupported.

`src/components/Hero/Background/liquid-settings.ts` holds the saved flow settings (size 45, force 15, persistence 57, resistance 2.4) with the Clear water preset's optical values. The desktop renderer loads dynamically. Its low-resolution fluid simulation settles after input stops; the logo composite continues animating while visible. Resizes preserve the marquee phase.

The desktop logo strip stays flat across its full width, with no edge bending or automatic water sheen. Signed scroll velocity accelerates it and adds premultiplied horizontal blur to the logos only. Down scrolls in the original direction; up reverses it. After input settles, speed eases back to the CSS-derived base speed in the most recent direction. The heading and rule stay stationary and sharp. “Marquee scroll acceleration” and “Marquee motion blur” are adjustable under `?heroSettings`. The liquid cursor retains its saved settings and layer toggle. Mobile keeps its original CSS marquee.

The marquee's stationary `h2` and top border are rasterised into a separate texture from their computed CSS. Their viewport position, responsive spacing, border width/colour, heading background, padding, font and letter spacing come from the HTML layout; only the logo strip scrolls. The renderer waits for fonts, refreshes on layout/content/theme changes and font loading, and cleans up its observers on unmount. This is a targeted renderer for the marquee's single-line heading and solid top border, not a general HTML-to-canvas engine.

The desktop “Liquid controls” toggle opens a lazily loaded panel with live brush, flow, trail, turbulence, volume and optical controls. “Show in front of portrait” switches the portrait into the refracted scene; switching it off restores the water behind the portrait. In front is the saved default, with highlight strength 0.78. Closing the panel retains the current settings; reloading or “Reset saved defaults” restores the saved defaults. The controls are hidden on mobile, coarse-pointer and reduced-motion layouts. The panel does not feed pointer or scroll gestures into the hero.

The renderer shares GSAP's ticker, pauses when the hero is offscreen or the tab is hidden, caches the portrait once its motion settles, and releases listeners and GPU resources on unmount. Without the desktop marquee it skips settled frames entirely. Canvas resolution is capped at 1.5× device pixels. Reduced-motion visitors see the original static portrait. Failed image loading or unavailable/lost WebGL also leaves the static image visible; restored contexts rebuild the renderer.

Assets are in `public/images/hero-depth/`: the transparent 4046×2799 source portrait and the 1508×1043 generated depth map. The portrait is served through `next/image` (responsive AVIF/WebP), and the WebGL texture reuses the fallback `<img>` element, so each visitor downloads one viewport-sized copy. The source PNG is never sent to the browser. The depth map is a single-channel lossless WebP, with values exactly matching the original red channel. The local nose-depth correction is baked into the depth texture once on the CPU (`bakeDepth` in `renderer.ts`), so the ray march reads one depth texel per step. This is a single-image depth illusion; large parallax and zoom settings can reveal approximation artifacts.
